# Contractor IP Assignment & Confidentiality Agreement (Template)

> **Step 22 — Brand / IP protection.** This template ensures Swan Match, Inc. legally owns all
> work product created by contractors and offshore developers. **Have every contractor (past and
> present) sign this before they touch code, designs, or company accounts.** This is the single most
> important IP document for an early-stage app with outside developers — without it, ownership of the
> codebase can be disputed, which scares away investors and acquirers.
>
> ⚠️ This is a starting template, not legal advice. Have a US attorney finalize it before signing.

---

**INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR — IP ASSIGNMENT & CONFIDENTIALITY AGREEMENT**

This Agreement is entered into as of ____________ (the "Effective Date") by and between:

- **Company:** Swan Match, Inc., a corporation organized under the laws of the State of California ("Company"), and
- **Contractor:** ____________________ ("Contractor"), of ____________________ (address).

### 1. Services
Contractor will provide software development and related services to the Company as described in
one or more statements of work or written instructions (the "Services").

### 2. Assignment of Intellectual Property
Contractor agrees that all work product, deliverables, source code, designs, documentation,
inventions, and materials created by Contractor in connection with the Services (collectively,
"Work Product") are **"works made for hire"** and are the **sole and exclusive property of the Company**.
To the extent any Work Product does not qualify as a work made for hire, Contractor hereby
**irrevocably assigns** to the Company all worldwide right, title, and interest in and to the Work Product,
including all copyrights, patents, trademarks, trade secrets, and other intellectual property rights.

### 3. Further Assurances
Contractor agrees to execute any documents and take any actions reasonably requested by the Company
to perfect, register, or enforce the Company's ownership of the Work Product, at the Company's expense.

### 4. Moral Rights
Contractor waives any "moral rights" or similar rights in the Work Product to the extent permitted by law.

### 5. Confidentiality
Contractor will hold in strict confidence all non-public information of the Company, including source
code, business plans, user data, credentials, API keys, financials, and roadmaps ("Confidential
Information"), and will not disclose or use it except to perform the Services. This obligation survives
termination indefinitely for trade secrets and for **three (3) years** for other Confidential Information.

### 6. Data Protection
Contractor will not copy, export, or retain any user personal data outside Company-approved systems,
and will comply with the Company's Privacy Policy and applicable data-protection laws. On termination,
Contractor will delete all copies of Confidential Information and user data in their possession.

### 7. No Pre-Existing or Third-Party IP
Contractor represents that the Work Product is original and does not infringe any third party's rights,
and that Contractor will not incorporate any third-party or open-source code without written disclosure
of its license to the Company.

### 8. Return of Materials & Credentials
On termination, Contractor will return or delete all Company materials and will surrender access to all
Company accounts, repositories, and credentials. Contractor will not retain admin access to Firebase,
Stripe, GitHub, the App Store/Play Console, the domain registrar, or email after the engagement ends.

### 9. Independent Contractor Status
Contractor is an independent contractor, not an employee, and is responsible for their own taxes.

### 10. Governing Law
This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of California, without regard to conflict-of-laws rules.

## How to use this (checklist)

- [ ] **Get every current and past developer to sign**, including anyone who built the original code.
- [ ] Store signed copies in a secure shared drive (see `docs/brand/Brand_Asset_Ownership_Register.md`).
- [ ] On offboarding, run the access-removal steps in §8.
